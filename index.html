What just happened?

When you clicked "Wave", your browser sent a tiny message across your Wi-Fi network. That message traveled at the speed of light — 186,000 miles per second — to your robot's brain, an ESP32 microchip no bigger than a postage stamp.

The robot's brain decoded your message and sent an electric pulse to a servo motor — a tiny motor that can rotate to an exact angle. That's how it raised its paw. One click, four miracles of engineering.

Wi-Fi: Your invisible superpower

Wi-Fi is just radio waves — the same kind of waves that carry music to your car radio. But instead of music, Wi-Fi carries data: text, images, commands.

The brain inside your robot

Your robot runs on an ESP32 — a chip that costs about $3 yet has enough power to connect to Wi-Fi, read sensors, play sounds, and control four legs simultaneously.

ESP32
📶
Wi-Fi + Bluetooth Talks to your phone and the internet

160 MHz processor Runs 160 million operations per second
🎵
Speaker + Microphone Can hear you and talk back

💡 Fun fact: The first computer that guided Apollo astronauts to the Moon had less processing power than this $3 chip in your robot.

The same tech, everywhere

Mars Rovers

NASA sends commands to Perseverance the same way you just controlled your robot — a message across a network. Theirs just travels 140 million miles.

Surgical Robots

Satellites
